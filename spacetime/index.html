Temporal-Spatial Transposition

Slice a video through time

A video is a block of pixels in (x, y, time). Play it normally and you see one moment at a time. Slice the same block along x or y instead, and time becomes a direction you can see: motion turns into shape. Pick an example, upload a clip, or record yourself.

How it works

The clip is stacked into a 3D array, frames × height × width. Each view reads that same block along a different axis.

X-Y-T fixes time: the normal video.

Y-T-X fixes a column x, like a photo-finish camera. Time runs left to right: things moving up or down leave slanted streaks, and things crossing sideways get stretched or squashed by their speed.

T-X-Y fixes a row y. Time runs top to bottom, so anything moving sideways leaves a streak whose slope is its speed: the travelling wave becomes diagonal stripes. The orbiting dot only shows up when it crosses the row, cutting the helix into points; sweep the row and they trace it out.

This is a browser port of my Python tool, which does the same with NumPy and OpenCV.
